Two automation figures, and why both matter. 96.5% of loads end up filed. 51.9% get there without anyone touching them. Most of the rest were held for a while and then cleared by Cerbi itself, usually because an invoice and its bill of lading (BOL) arrive separately and the first one waits for the second.

3. Holds

October's loads were held 10,731 times. How those holds ended:

OutcomeHoldsShare
Cleared by Cerbi (a later check let it go)4,62143%
Replaced by a re-read4,58843%
Cleared by a person1,12210%
Still open3453%
Discarded551%

So most holds are waiting, not work: a person cleared about one hold in ten. A load can be held more than once, so these are holds, not loads.

The biggest and slowest hold is "no matching load in the ERP after a re-read": 3,123 holds, median 47 hours to clear, 90th percentile 161 hours. Cerbi cleared 2,448 of them itself when the matching load was entered in the ERP. It is the main reason a held document waits a median 47.5 hours in Purgatory, and shortening it is a question about when loads reach the ERP, not about reading.

5. Where the model spend goes

Shares only. Telematics documents arrive already typed and cost nothing to read.

Read pathDocumentsShare of spend

About 2% of documents, the large grouped packets, take 36% of the spend. A cap on parts, or a cheaper first pass for them, is where cost would move. A batch read costs about 27% less a document than a single read.
